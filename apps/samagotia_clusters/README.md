# Samagotia Clusters

Standalone BMW iDrive 7 app with one F8X cluster design. Its F8X runtime, settings page, vehicle decoder and artwork are independent of the sample Cluster Themes app. It uses the credited MGU App Kit infrastructure and requires the separately installed `cluster-render` 0.5.3 or later.

Build from the studio root with `python3 tools/build_clusters.py`. Use `--stage-only` for the Mac preview stage. A normal build uses an isolated temporary stage and creates a Store package plus dependency catalog. No command here deploys to a vehicle.

The parked-test build starts with the BMW cluster selected, disables simulated data on the car, and returns to the BMW cluster if speed or RPM is missing or stale. Renderer disconnection also requires manual reselection after recovery. Live RPM and oil-temperature field decoding are implemented, but have not been tested on a G82. The oil-temperature needle uses the live oil reading in Celsius. Oil temperature scale defaults to Auto (vehicle model), using the existing fresh vehicle identity: known G8x models select 50–100–150 °C and known F8x models select 70–120–170 °C. Explicit scale choices remain available. Unknown or unavailable identity uses a labelled 50–150 °C fallback. The settings row shows Auto with the model or Fallback. Fahrenheit faces retain Celsius needle calibration. These are model-selected display profiles, not a transmitted gauge-limit or normal-temperature-range signal. Printed values are scale marks, not inferred engine safety thresholds. RPM limit source defaults to Auto (BMW signal, experimental), using `de.bmw.infrastructure.VehicleInformation.DataPowerTrain2CRC.rPMEngineMaximumAllowed` notifications and the mapped 50 RPM/count decode. The field and byte encoding are source-mapped; availability, physical scaling and cold-redline tracking still need confirmation on each car, including the G82. A fresh, recognised stock-model identity supplies a plausibility check: values above that model's nominal specification are rejected with a diagnostic (for example, 8150 exceeds G82 stock 7200), and the shift band stays hidden. The rejected value is not replaced with a claimed live profile value. Auto also requires a fresh recognised vehicle profile; losing that identity keeps the band hidden rather than reviving an unchecked candidate. Its status labels the candidate unverified. The subscription requests notifications only, with no invented getter. A conservative three-second freshness window is enforced; the SOME/IP delivery rate itself has not been measured on the G82. Missing, invalid, stale or disconnected limit data hides the entire warning band and disables shift-light building/flashing. A configured redline is used only after explicitly selecting Manual. The moving oil-based warmup estimate is SIM ONLY and never substitutes for BMW limit data on the car. Parking-brake status has a mapped live API, without a G82 capture confirming it is populated. Full-pedal and optional vehicle fields still require verification on each car.

Settings shows the limit source, decoded RPM, raw byte, freshness and provider state for the parked check. Older firmware without the `DataPowerTrain2CRC` catalog field may require installer compatibility handling before this build can install.

The visible options are F8X design, speed/distance units, temperature units, oil temperature scale, digital speed, gear number in D, backlight colour (Original or Amber), information page, bottom row, date format, clock, time zone, fuel tank size, RPM limit source, explicit manual redline, and shift-point offset. Hidden `demo` and `coldGuard` fields exist only for the separate Mac test bench; no simulator ships in this app.

BMW retains its turn, parking-brake and other stock warning telltales. The app creates no custom turn arrows or parking-brake light, and provides no preference that can enable duplicates. Historical turn/brake preferences are ignored, and saved duplicate widgets are excluded before layer creation. Native telltale placement and warning priority are not changed.

Sequential up/down recommendation arrows read `CDS.statusGearIndicator.displayGearShiftIndicatorDrive`: `HOCHSCHALTHINWEIS`/2 means up, `RUNTERSCHALTHINWEIS`/1 means down, and off/invalid hides the arrows. The field and enums are verified in the owner's catalog; G82 delivery still needs a stock-display comparison. The icons sit beside the large gear only with a fresh direction, a valid forward gear and connected MDrive S/M program. Automatic, P/N/R, stale, unsupported, provider-loss and demo states hide them. Direction expires after 1.5 seconds and has a 500 ms getter; program state belongs to the notifier's provider session and reconnect needs a fresh program notification. No RPM-based recommendation or ambiguous gearbox `dummy` field is used. Pixels are cached; live changes update visibility only.

Cruise set-speed indication reads only `DisplayDataServer2.functionalLibraryData`. The marker on the custom speedometer is green when active and orange in standby, and uses the same curve as the needle with explicit MPH-to-km/h conversion. Unsupported modes, stale data, provider loss or a missing speed unit hide it. The app omits its standalone cruise-status symbol under the no-duplicate-telltale policy. This omission is conservative; a retained stock cruise symbol position above the G82 panel has not been verified. Server2 delivery and live MPH accuracy still require car verification.

Day needle cast shadows use separate shaft-shaped textures with a short soft edge and fixed screen-space lighting offsets. The shadows rotate with the smoothed needles; blade overlap changes their visible side naturally. Night retains its original shadow textures and lighting.

Original copyrights and licences are retained in `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.

The RPM mapping is documented in the [public BMW M8 i-Level 20-07 catalog](https://github.com/user-attachments/files/34052657/someip-interfaces.json) and corroborated against the [F-series CAN maximum-RPM definition](https://github.com/billyjack2/CAN-DBC-Collection/blob/6e2f88fd6cd9c1f47d0db7bd022711b106f27027/BMW/F_Series/can1.dbc). The catalog establishes the field, not per-vehicle delivery or cold-limit behavior.

Backlight colour is a local visual preference, independent of BMW signals. Original is the default and retains the saved artwork. Amber warms only numbers, ticks, scale rails, unit labels and the fuel/oil symbols in Day and Night. Metal, hub lighting, red needles, warning colours and information text retain their original finish. The setting replaces four resident face images in place; motion performs no colour conversion or texture upload.

The plain lower tachometer arc completes the open ring beneath the gear indicator. It is a static native effect aligned to the actual tachometer pivot and scale, with grey Day/Night colours and no extra image allocation or vehicle-state inference. Day rims use a restrained continuous directional steel finish across their thicker sections; Night metal artwork is unchanged.

Fuel reserve indication reads `KombiIF.statusRangeTankStatus.statusFluidLevelFuelTankSpare` from the existing live stream. Fresh `AKTIV`/1 shows the information-panel message and pump graphic; fresh `NICHT_AKTIV`/0 clears them. If that flag is absent/invalid, valid live range at or below 50 km triggers the message regardless of display units. Provider loss or a sample age of 25 seconds hides unknown data; reconnect requires a fresh sample. The generic attention-triangle replica is omitted under the no-duplicate-telltale policy. The pump remains part of the custom information panel, whose stock gauge content is covered by the opaque floor.


## Information pages (0.15.0)

Selectable pages are Off, Outside temperature, Date, Average fuel consumption, Range remaining, Time and Average speed. With digital speed off, outside temperature and time stay in the two fixed rows; duplicate selectable entries are suppressed. With digital speed on, Time and Outside temperature can be selected individually. The custom speed + clock + consumption arrangement is retained. Fresh fuel-reserve warnings temporarily select numeric range without changing the saved page; clearing/expiring the warning restores it. This custom priority uses the same independently freshness-checked warning as the information-panel fuel message.

Temperature, distance, 12/24-hour clock, date order/separator and fuel economy default to Follow car. Explicit preferences override them. `Vehicle.statusNewCurrentUnitsStatusExt` is polled every ten seconds; missing preferences use metric, 24-hour and D.M.Y styling where needed, with a settings notice. Fuel economy uses the packet's explicit unit if the car preference is unavailable; UK and US gallons are never conflated. Exterior Celsius preserves half-degree resolution, and odometer/trip formatting retains leading zeroes.

Time/date defaults to `KombiIF.statusShowTimeDate`, polled every second. Car-local components are displayed directly, without applying the Mac/head-unit timezone again. Missing or invalid time shows `--`; a supported clock with unknown date-year encoding can still show valid time. Full literal calendar years are accepted; offset/two-digit years are rejected rather than guessing a century. The catalog supplies date order and separator, **not year width**; automatic F8X dates use two-digit years. System clock is an explicit alternative with its own timezone setting. None of these new readers has been tested on the G82.

Range, fuel litres, odometer and average readings expire after 25 seconds; exterior temperature after five seconds. Invalid fields and provider loss clear independently. Average speed/consumption use the verified 0.1 scale and 4095 invalid sentinel, including numeric or named unit enums. Stationary valid zeroes remain readings, not missing data.

The independently resettable factory trip counter is **not verified**. `wayPassed` is a travel-computer value, so its unused production subscription has been removed; it is not shown as the factory trip. On the car the trip slot shows `--`; the Mac supplies an explicitly simulated separate trip fixture for layout review. BC stalk input, current-consumption physical scaling, and a complete cross-car SLI decoder remain unverified and are not connected. No trip-reset command is sent. Navigation/Waze remains deferred. Evidence and remaining validation are in `docs/F8X-info-page-signal-gaps.md` in the source project.


## Door, bonnet and boot display (0.16.0)

The lower-right warning area now supports a small original top-down silver car with amber open side doors and red bonnet/boot sections. The message sits beneath the tachometer arc; gear, main dials and central BC readouts remain in place. Two-/four-door silhouettes are selected by a conservative model/body mapping from `Vehicle2.statusVehiclePartType`. Vehicle settings provides Automatic, 2 doors or 4 doors and Automatic/LHD/RHD steering overrides. These are display policies, not a wire-provided door count or exact model-specific 3D artwork. An SUV uses the four-door symbolic profile. Physical left/right requires known steering side or an explicit override; unknown side data produces generic text without an invented closed-car diagram.

`DoorsWindowsRoofMirrors.CentralLockingSystemAndPanelStatus` and `CentralLockingSystemAndPanelStatus2` are notification-only. Exact contact enums map to open, closed or unknown; no getter, lock/unlock command or short expiry timer is invented. States belong to the provider session, and provider loss/reconnect clears contacts until another sample. The first meaningful Status2 packet owns the whole snapshot for that session; an initial all-invalid Status2 cannot hide valid legacy data. Metadata uses a real getter every ten seconds and expires independently after sixty seconds. Delivery cadence and physical side semantics still need checking on the G82. Contact states are binary, not measured door angles. A secondary boot contact produces a generic tailgate warning; no upper/lower split-tailgate meaning is assumed.

The shared presenter uses app precedence Hood open, Boot open, Tailgate open, Door(s) open, then Fuel reserve. All confirmed drawable openings remain highlighted even when another closure owns the text. Only the referenced hood warning uses red text; side-door and boot labels are pale. Closing doors restores a still-active fuel message and pump graphic. Numeric low-fuel range priority remains independent. These rules are not claimed to duplicate BMW Check Control arbitration or hide timers. No all-closed green reassurance is shown from missing data.

The diagram is one resident 72×116 RGBA image, counted twice by the renderer (~0.064 MiB). Its bounded cache and state key avoid re-rasterizing or uploading it while unchanged; needle animation remains independent. Vehicle settings update the diagram without rebuilding needle layers. The default layout has about 30 layers and 8.6 MiB image memory.

BMW door/Check Control popups may take the panel slot. The app changes no native ownership, yielding or takeover flags; it must not fight BMW to keep its replica visible. The Mac proves our decoding, priority, pixels and fallback logic, not G82 reception or native warning visibility. No car has been connected for this feature.

Mac controls are under **Simulated signals → Doors / panels (SIM)**: G82/G80/G05/unknown, steering side, provider availability and every closure. These feed car-shaped packets into the same reader as production. Test examples: a passenger front door in G82/LHD, the same input in RHD, G80 with both rear doors, bonnet plus boot, unknown steering, and provider loss/recovery while a warning is active. The Mac alone also supplies the explicitly simulated mileage trip fixture described above.


## G82 installation compatibility (0.16.1)

Exterior temperature requests notification permission only (`s`). The connected G82's interface catalog exposes `ExteriorTemperature.notifier` with no getter; version 0.16.0 incorrectly requested `rs`, which the Store refused before installing the app. The runtime subscribes without polling. Missing, invalid, unavailable or stale temperature still shows `--`; notification delivery has not yet been verified in our running app. The complete generated manifest is checked locally against the connected head unit's catalog before publication; that catalog is kept private and is not distributed. This validates declared capabilities, not live signal delivery or native warning takeover.


## Opaque panel backing (0.16.2)

The Day and Night backgrounds are opaque across the full 1920×720 panel. The painted falloff is composited over black in the asset so it keeps its appearance without exposing the BMW gauges and readouts underneath. The bake tool preserves this opacity. No exclusive layers, renderer ownership, warning-yield or takeover settings are changed. Off, stale speed/RPM, renderer disconnect and app shutdown still remove all our layers so the BMW display can return. Native warning takeover and the visible result still require a parked check on the car; an opaque background alone does not prove BMW warning visibility.


## Needle motion and settings load (0.16.8)

The cluster renderer keeps face and needle textures resident on the GPU. The app submits the newest measured RPM/speed angle on the next eligible frame, with at most 60 output frames per second. The extra 36 ms fast-needle easing has been removed. Needle, shadow and glass poses for each connection share one bounded socket write. Fuel and oil retain 100 ms smoothing with a lower pose-command cadence. All companion shadows and glass use the same sampled angle. There is no per-reading native animation replacement, future RPM prediction or moving-needle bitmap upload. This avoids the upstream RAMSES 19 animation-system rebuild triggered by replacement. Command cadence is not measured display FPS.

RPM-only fallback getters run every 10 ms and speed-only backup getters every 50 ms. Unchanged critical readings refresh freshness without redundant render callbacks. Digital speed and gear readouts use a 50 ms rendering gate instead of 200 ms. Notifications still deliver immediately. The signal helper allows one pending getter per field and prevents an older reply overriding a newer notification. The 10 ms request interval permits up to 100 attempts per second; one outstanding request bounds actual reads. Actual vehicle data freshness must be measured: polling a cached value faster cannot create newer engine data.

Backpressure pauses motion work and clears stale interpolation. Socket drain resumes at the latest real sample; delayed timers do not replay missed frames. Off, stale speed/RPM, disconnect and shutdown remove the complete custom display. A full-panel opaque effect beneath the decorative background needs no image upload, covering the BMW gauges while a background image is pending. BMW-controlled warning and indicator telltales remain above the app's panel.

The central iDrive settings page records a new canvas only on a visible change or user event, checking live diagnostics at most once per second. The guard precedes canvas recording because a GPU-backed loop can run at display refresh regardless of its requested fallback rate.

The [0.16.8 deterministic runtime comparison](../../docs/f8x-motion-performance-0.16.8.json) models the same 20 Hz RPM source against 0.16.7: mean tracking lag falls from 57.61 ms to 24.49 ms. This excludes real car transport, scene flush, GPU timing and physical display refresh. It does not establish a G82 frame rate or responsiveness result. A hypothetical genuinely fresh 40 Hz source helps further; reading the same cached 20 Hz value twice as often does not.

Parked feedback reports stable display in BMW's normal cluster view and repeated stock/custom switching in M display view. Use normal view for these tests; M-view compatibility and BMW warning takeover are not established.


The custom panel keeps its large gear readout, speed/range/temperature information, cruise set-speed marker, fuel-reserve text/pump, door-opening illustration/text and verified sequential shift recommendation. Those are base-panel information, not replacements for BMW’s turn, parking-brake, seat-belt, engine or other native status telltales. Custom turn, parking-brake, standalone cruise-status and generic attention-triangle widgets are rejected even if an older saved layout or preference names them. The owner photo proves duplicate turn arrows and the retained stock parking-brake light; no claim is made that every other G82 icon’s native layer has been verified.
